Goals

Our Vision

OpenCIVAN aims to establish a sustainable open-source ecosystem that enables people in different locations to collaboratively visualize and analyze complex scientific data in immersive environments.

1

Advance Immersive Visualization and Analytics

Develop methods and software that allow users to inspect multidimensional scientific data through interactive XR and CAVE environments. The project will support richer spatial reasoning and more direct interaction with scientific models, observations, simulations, and forecasts.

2

Enable Geographically Distributed Collaboration

Provide shared environments in which researchers and domain experts can communicate, coordinate analysis, compare perspectives, and work together in real time even when they are not physically co-located.

3

Connect Immersive Interfaces with Networked Computing

Integrate immersive visualization with cloud services, WebXR, WebAssembly, WebGPU, and HPC resources. This connection is intended to support large datasets, remote computation, and deployment across a range of devices and facilities.

4

Lower Barriers to Adoption

Create documentation, tutorials, examples, and reusable software components that make immersive visualization more accessible to researchers, educators, developers, and institutions with different levels of technical experience and available resources.

5

Build an Extensible Open-Source Foundation

Develop a modular ecosystem that can support ParaView-based applications as well as future integrations with other immersive visualization and analytics systems, including SlicerVirtual-Reality, IATK, and Vrui.

6

Foster a Sustainable Contributor Community

Establish transparent governance, clear contribution pathways, community events, mentorship opportunities, and long-term plans for maintenance, funding, and technical development.

Guiding Principles

Openness

Software, documentation, and development processes should remain accessible to the community.

Inclusivity

The ecosystem should welcome contributors and users from different disciplines, institutions, backgrounds, and levels of experience.

Interoperability

Open standards and modular interfaces should support multiple devices, platforms, and visualization applications.

Reproducibility

Open code, documented workflows, and testable releases should support repeatable scientific and technical work.

Sustainability

Community health, maintainability, governance, and long-term resources are part of the technical design.

Intended Impact

OpenCIVAN is intended to support more effective scientific discovery, collaborative problem solving, immersive education, and access to advanced visualization capabilities. Its open-source model can also help under-resourced institutions participate in research and innovation without depending exclusively on proprietary platforms.
